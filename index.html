Method User guideMethodDevelopment

Slices

Each pixel is the equal-weight mean of the unique finite, unmasked voxels in the slab, pooled with all their symmetry equivalents. No smoothing or interpolation; grey means no measured contribution.

Symmetry

Generators are closed into a group, and every operation must map bin centers onto bin centers. A warning appears when the operations change the cell metric by more than 2%. Oblique axes use the reciprocal metric from the UB matrix.

Mask

Edge erosion removes measured voxels near unmeasured ones, where detector edges leave spurious values; the outlier cut removes voxels far above the median of their equivalents (1.4826·MAD). The mask acts before averaging.

3-D view

The volume is binned by an odd factor with blocks aligned to the origin, symmetrized and masked the same way, and drawn as a transparent isosurface with the current slices as planes.

I(Q)

Each shell's intensity is the mean over the voxels with data in it, so gaps and masks do not lower it. Symmetry orbits count with their multiplicity, voxels are split into sub-cells shared between shells, and σ is propagated from the file's uncertainties.

Isosurface grid

I(Q)
Q bins (ΔQ field)

0.05: shells of 0.05 Å⁻¹ from Q min to Q max. -0.01: logarithmic shells, ΔQ/Q = 1%. 0.5, 0.02, 3, 0.05, 10: 0.02 Å⁻¹ from 0.5 to 3, then 0.05 to 10. As in Mantid's Rebin, a range ends with a bin of 0.25 to 1.25 steps. The slider changes a single step and recomputes as it moves.

Split voxels

Each voxel is divided into n³ sub-cells, binned by their own |Q|, so a voxel is shared between the shells it overlaps. Binning whole voxels by their centres aliases when ΔQ is close to the voxel size; 3³ takes about twice as long as 2³.

Drag to zoom (a flat drag zooms Q only), double-click for the full range. In Move mode, drag to pan.

Line cut

In Cut mode, drag across a slice to draw a cut, and drag an end to change it; hold Shift to keep it along a lattice direction. The cut lies in its slice's plane and follows the slice's position. Each point averages the voxels in a rod of diameter W around the line, pooled with their symmetry equivalents as in the slices.
